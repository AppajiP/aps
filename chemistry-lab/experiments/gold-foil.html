Rutherford's gold foil experiment

Fire α-particles at a thin sheet of gold foil and detect them on a surrounding fluorescent screen. The pattern of scattering reveals the existence of the atomic nucleus.

What it shows

  • Most α-particles pass straight through — atoms are mostly empty space.
  • A few are deflected at large angles — there is a small, dense, positively-charged region.
  • Very rarely (~1 in 8000) an α-particle bounces back — direct hit on the nucleus.

This led Rutherford in 1911 to propose the nuclear model of the atom, replacing J. J. Thomson's "plum-pudding" model.

Geometry

The cross-section for back-scattering scales as Z²/E², which is why a heavy nucleus such as gold (Z = 79) gives an observable rebound rate.
