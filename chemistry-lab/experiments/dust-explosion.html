Flour-dust explosion in a sealed can

Inside a closed paint can, a small candle ignites a cloud of fine flour stirred up by a hand-pump. Pressure surges and the loose lid blows off.

Why fine powder explodes

A pile of flour merely smoulders because oxygen can only reach the outside surface. Spread the same flour into a fine cloud and the surface-area-to-mass ratio jumps thousands of times. Combustion can then propagate as a flame front across the entire dust cloud in milliseconds — that is an explosion.

Real-world hazard

Grain silos, sugar mills, and coal mines have all suffered devastating dust explosions. Modern facilities use earthing, humidity control, and venting to mitigate the risk.
