How much of the air is oxygen?

Burn red phosphorus in a sealed bell-jar standing in water; the water rises by about one-fifth of the jar volume.

Sealed jar over water

Observations

Phase
ready
Water risen
0 %
O₂ remaining
21 %

Ignite the phosphorus to begin.

What happens

Excess red phosphorus reacts with the oxygen in the trapped air to form solid white phosphorus(V) oxide:

4 P + 5 O₂  →  2 P₂O₅

Because the product is a solid, the gas pressure inside drops and water rises through the clamp to refill the missing volume.

Why phosphorus?

  • Burns readily in air.
  • Product is a non-gaseous solid — pressure really drops.
  • No carbon dioxide is produced (it would partially dissolve in water).

Common errors

  • Too little phosphorus — not all O₂ is consumed → water rises less than ⅕.
  • Jar not sealed quickly — outside air leaks in.
  • Jar opened before it has cooled — hot gas contracts on cooling.
